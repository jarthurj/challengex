How these summaries are made and checked

Gemini (or, for two older ones, a rule-based template) sees only the published values for one subject, the changes between them, the statistical projection and its error, and the methodology notes. Before a summary is stored, and again every time a page shows it, every number in it is checked against the data: each is a published value, change, labelled projection or backtest error of the metric it cites, for the household group named nearest to it and the fiscal year it is given for; rises and falls match the data; changes in shares are in percentage points; comparisons across methodology breaks name them; and the headline and caveats carry no figures. A summary that fails is never shown. The check catches most numeric errors, not every wrong sentence, so each summary says whether a person has reviewed it.
